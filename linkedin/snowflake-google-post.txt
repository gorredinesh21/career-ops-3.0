Two summits. One message: "you won't need data engineers soon."

I spent some time this week going through what Snowflake and Google announced at their summits, and the direction is hard to miss.

Snowflake is pushing an "Agentic Enterprise" — Cortex Code writing pipelines on its own, agents that migrate legacy systems, AI that runs feature engineering and training from a plain English prompt.

Google went even further. Their BigQuery Data Engineering Agent is now GA. You type "load this bucket, standardize the dates, dedupe on order ID, write to clean_orders" — and it generates the SQL, the tests, and the docs. At scale. Across hundreds of pipelines.

As someone who builds these pipelines for a living, my honest first reaction was: this is genuinely impressive. My second reaction was: I don't think it eliminates the data engineer. At least not yet. Here's why I think that 👇

1. The hard part of data engineering was never the code. It's knowing what "clean" even means for your business. Which duplicate is the real one. Why a number is "wrong" when it's technically valid. An agent can write the join. It can't tell you that finance defines revenue differently from sales.

2. Context lives in people's heads, not in the schema. Half my job is figuring out why a source system behaves the way it does, who owns it, and what breaks downstream if I touch it. That tribal knowledge isn't in any prompt.

3. Accountability doesn't automate. When a pipeline silently corrupts a dashboard the CEO sees, someone has to own it, debug it, and explain it. "The agent generated it" is not an answer anyone accepts.

4. Garbage prompt → confident garbage. These tools are only as good as the person specifying the requirement. Which… still sounds a lot like needing a data engineer, just one who now writes intent instead of boilerplate.

So my honest take: the boring parts of our job are getting automated, and that's a good thing. The role shifts from "writing pipelines" to "deciding what's correct, governing it, and owning the outcome."

But here's the part I won't pretend about — when both Snowflake and Google are betting their roadmaps on this, I've learned not to say "impossible." These are companies that turn multi-year bets into reality. The line keeps moving. What needs a human today might not in a few years.

So I'm not threatened by it. I'm learning it. Because the engineer who knows how to direct these agents will be far more valuable than the one who refused to look.

What's your take — augmentation or replacement? 👇
